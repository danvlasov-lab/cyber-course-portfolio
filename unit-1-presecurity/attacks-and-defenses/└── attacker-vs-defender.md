## Goal
To practice thinking like both an attacker and a defender about a system you actually use. This is the dual mindset Section 7 introduces - and it's the lens I'll be wearing for the rest of the course.

## Attacker vs Defender - My personal email account

### The system
My personal email is really important to me because there are many accounts linked to it for various services. For example, there are many accounts on gaming services that are linked to my personal email and that are really important to me.

### Attacker view
An attacker may try to steal my email password by sending me a phishing link. For example, someone I know can send me a link to a gaming site and tell me that it is safe. Since I trust this person, I can follow the link and enter my email address and password on the fake website. An attacker can then use these credentials to access my email and possibly other accounts connected to it.

### Defender view
I would first check an unfamiliar link before opening it. For example, I could copy the link and ask ChatGPT to analyze the URL and explain if it looks suspicious. Then I would decide whether to open the website or not. I would also use multi-factor authentication on my email account, so a single stolen password would not be enough to access it. However, checking the link using artificial intelligence cannot guarantee its security, and attackers can still use other ways to trick me.

### CIA angle
The main threat is the violation of confidentiality. The attacker's main goal is to get my email address, password, and possibly access to other accounts associated with my email.

### What I'm changing this week
This week, I will enable multi-factor authentication in my personal email account. I will also check unfamiliar links before opening them and avoid entering an email password on websites that are unknown to me. This will reduce the risk of losing access to my email, even if my password is hacked.
