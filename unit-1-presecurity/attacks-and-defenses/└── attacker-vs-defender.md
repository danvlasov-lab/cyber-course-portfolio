## Attacker vs Defender — My personal email account

### The system
My personal email is really important for me, because a lot of accounts are linked to this for various services. For example, there are lots of accounts on game services, which are linked on my personal email and really matters for me.

### Attacker view
For instance, an acquaintance of mine was coerced—willingly or not—into cybercrime, and his initial assignment was to steal data from some unsuspecting person so he could climb the criminal ladder. Harboring a grudge against me for past grievances, he decides to make me his first victim. Without revealing his true intentions, he might—during a casual conversation—suggest I visit a "safe" link (say, to a gaming site) and sign up, insisting it is a top-notch, reliable source. He could provide this link from a third-party site or one of his own that contains code designed to harvest user information. If I were to click the link and register—a scenario quite likely given the trust I place in him—he could obtain my email address and password, allowing him to use the account however he pleased.

### Defender view
Personally, I would first make sure the link is safe. I’d copy it and send it to, say, ChatGPT. Then, based on what it told me about the link my acquaintance sent, I’d decide what to do next. If the link is completely safe, I’d click on it without hesitation. If it turns out to be fake, I could confront my acquaintance about it. He might try to convince me the link is safe—claiming the source is one in a million and the best around—or even play the guilt card: "Don't you trust me? We've known each other for so long!" Alternatively, he might just back down for the moment, claiming ignorance ("I didn't know! Someone else sent it to me!"), but he would likely keep targeting me over the long haul, trying to steal my data. Unfortunately, I wouldn't be able to fend off his attempts forever; and eventually, I’d end up clicking that malicious link, and he’d steal my data.
