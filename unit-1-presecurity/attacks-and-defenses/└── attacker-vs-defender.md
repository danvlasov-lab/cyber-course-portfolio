## Goal
To practice thinking like both an attacker and a defender about a system you actually use. This is the dual mindset Section 7 introduces - and it's the lens I'll be wearing for the rest of the course.

## Attacker vs Defender — My personal email account

### The system
My personal email is really important for me, because a lot of accounts are linked to this for various services. For example, there are lots of accounts on game services, which are linked on my personal email and really matters for me.

### Attacker view
An attacker could try to steal my email password by sending me a phishing link. For example, someone I know could send me a link to a gaming website and tell me that it is safe. Because I trust this person, I might click the link and enter my email and password on the fake website. The attacker could then use these credentials to access my email and possibly other accounts connected to it.

### Defender view
I would first check an unfamiliar link before opening it. For example, I could copy the link and ask ChatGPT to analyze the URL and explain whether it looks suspicious. I would then decide whether to open the website or not. I would also use multi-factor authentication on my email account, so a stolen password alone would not be enough to access it. However, checking a link with AI cannot guarantee that it is safe, and attackers could still use other ways to trick me.

### CIA angle
The primary threat is to Confidentiality. The attacker’s main goal is to obtain my email address, password, and potentially access to other accounts linked to my email.

### What I'm changing this week
This week, I will enable multi-factor authentication on my personal email account. I will also check unfamiliar links before opening them and avoid entering my email password on websites I do not recognize. This will reduce the risk of losing access to my email even if my password is compromised.
