## Findings

### 1. The incident in my own words

The WannaCry attack began on May 12th, 2017 and spread very quickly around the world. It was a ransomware that closed people's files and asked for a ransom. More than 200,000 computers in more than 150 companies were affected, including hospitals, companies, and government organizations. What surprised me the most was that WannaCry could spread from one computer to another automatically very quickly. He didn't wait for every user to click something.

### 2. Who was affected, and how

This is the National Health Service of Great Britain (NHS). Hospitals could not use their computer systems normally. Some appointments were cancelled and some patients had to go to other hospitals. In addition to hospitals, companies and government organizations were affected. This was especially serious because these are state-owned companies that are critically important for the country's infrastructure.

### 3. The CIA principle

I think the principle of availability and integrity has suffered the most. People couldn't get to their data and files and use their computers normally. The files were encrypted and modified, which also affects the principle of integrity. Users could not access their files normally.

### 4. The attack technique - ransomware and "wormable" exploits

Ransomware is malware that locks or encrypts files and asks people for ransom. Ordinary ransomware usually requires a person to click on something, but WannaCry could move from one computer to another automatically. WannaCry was very dangerous because it could spread itself using the EternalBlue vulnerability. This ability made the attack much more dangerous because WannaCry could infect other computers without user action and therefore spread very quickly.

### 5. How was it discovered and how was it stopped

People noticed the attack when their computers stopped working normally and displayed a ransom message. The message said that the files were encrypted and you need to pay a ransom in Bitcoin to restore them. Researcher Marcus Hutchins found the so-called **kill switch**. He registered a special domain that tested WannaCry, and after that, the spread of malware was slowed down and many new infections were stopped. This was unusual because the normal action of registering a single domain helped to partially stop a large cyberattack.

### 6. What could have helped - the patch question

Microsoft released a fix even before the attack. The reason was that some of the organizations used old computer systems that were more difficult to update. Blocking the network connection used by WannaCry would also help stop the spread. I would choose updates as the most important protection, because Microsoft has already released a fix for the vulnerability that WannaCry exploited. If organizations had installed the update on time, it could have helped protect systems.

### 7. The broader lesson

He showed me that software updates are very important for security. Old computers are especially dangerous in hospitals and other important organizations because they are more difficult and very inefficient to reset. I was surprised that a tool that was linked to government cyber capabilities later turned out to be in the hands of intruders and was used in a major cyberattack. This shows that such tools can become dangerous if they fall into the wrong hands. A common lesson for organizations is to always update their computer systems.

### 8. My personal takeaway

I was surprised that a state-owned company couldn't simply upgrade its old systems. I understand now that updates are a very important procedure that protects my software from threats. From now on, I will try to update my software on PCs and other devices to the latest versions and make more backups to save my data. The main idea is that you should always update to the latest version.
