## Findings

### 1. The incident in my own words

The WannaCry attack started on 12 May 2017 and spread very fast around the world. It was ransomware that locked people's files and asked them to pay money. About 200,000 computers in more than 150 countries were affected, including hospitals, companies and government organizations.

### 2. Who was affected, and how

Many organizations were affected by WannaCry. One of the biggest problems was in the UK National Health Service, where hospitals could not use their computer systems normally. Some appointments were cancelled and some patients had to go to other hospitals, so the attack affected real people, not only computers.

### 3. The CIA principle

I think **availability** was the main part of the CIA triad affected by WannaCry. People could not open their files or use their computers normally. The files were also changed because they were encrypted, but the biggest problem was that they were not available.

### 4. The attack technique - ransomware and “wormable” exploits

Ransomware is malware that locks or encrypts files and asks people to pay money. WannaCry was very dangerous because it could spread by itself using the EternalBlue vulnerability. Normal ransomware often needs a person to click on something, but WannaCry could move from one computer to another automatically.

### 5. How was it discovered and how was it stopped

People noticed the attack when their computers stopped working normally and showed a message asking for money. A security researcher found that the malware was trying to connect to a specific website. He registered the website, and this helped stop the attack from spreading, but infected computers were still infected.

### 6. What could have helped - the patch question

Microsoft had released a security update before the WannaCry attack, but many organizations did not install it. One reason was that some organizations used old computer systems that were difficult to update. Blocking the network connection used by WannaCry could also have helped to stop it from spreading.

### 7. The broader lesson

For me, WannaCry showed that software updates are very important for security. Old and unpatched computers can cause big problems, especially in hospitals and other important organizations. It also showed that government cyber-weapons can become dangerous when they are leaked and used by criminals.

### 8. My personal takeaway

After watching the documentary, I will take software updates more seriously. I now understand that updates can protect computers from serious attacks, not only add new features. I also think that backups are very important because they can help people get their files back after a ransomware attack.
