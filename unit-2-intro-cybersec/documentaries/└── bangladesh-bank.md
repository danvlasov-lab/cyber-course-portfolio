## Findings

### 1. The incident in your own words

The attackers wanted to steal about 1 billion dollars from Bangladesh Bank. They hacked the bank and used the SWIFT system to send money to other accounts. They stole about 81 million dollars. It was unusual because they robbed the bank without going inside the building.

### 2. The patient approach — months of preparation

The attackers were inside the bank's network for almost one year. During this time, they learned how the bank's computers and systems worked. They also prepared accounts where they could receive the stolen money. They were patient because they wanted to make the attack work.

### 3. The CIA principle

The main CIA principle was **integrity**. The attackers changed information in the bank's system and created fake payment orders. The system showed these orders as if they were real.

### 4. The attack technique — SWIFT and the printer trick

The attackers used SWIFT to send fake money transfer requests. They also stopped the printer from printing the transaction messages. Because of this, bank workers did not see the transfers quickly. This gave the attackers more time to move the money.

### 5. What went wrong for the attackers

The attackers tried to move about 951 million dollars, but they only got about 81 million dollars. Some transfers were stopped because there were mistakes in the messages, including a spelling mistake. Other banks also noticed problems and stopped some of the transfers. This shows that small mistakes and security checks can stop a large attack.

Better monitoring
