## Findings

### 1. Jeremy's story in my own words

Jeremy Hammond was a hacker. He hacked Stratfor and took private information. He was arrested and went to prison for about 10 years.

### 2. The target - Stratfor

Stratfor was a private intelligence company. Jeremy and his group thought that Stratfor was doing wrong things, so they wanted to expose the company. I understand their reason, but they still hacked the company without permission.

### 3. The legal reality

Jeremy was charged for accessing computer systems without permission. He spent about 10 years in federal prison. This shows that hacking can have serious legal consequences, even if the hacker thinks they have a good reason.

### 4. Activism, hacktivism, or crime?

I think Jeremy's actions were both activism and crime. He wanted to expose information for political reasons, but he still broke the law. Good intentions do not make hacking legal.

### 5. The "good intentions" defense

Good intentions are not enough to make hacking legal. A person can want to help but still cause damage or access private information. A security researcher needs permission before testing a system.

### 6. The technical concepts I noticed

I noticed the problem of password reuse. If a person uses the same password on different websites, a stolen password can be used to enter other accounts. This is why people should use different passwords.

### 7. The responsibility question - for me

I am learning cybersecurity, but I cannot use my skills on any system I want. I need permission before testing a system. If I find a serious security problem, I should report it to the owner. If I am not sure what to do, I should ask a teacher or a cybersecurity professional.

### 8. My personal takeaway

This episode showed me that cybersecurity skills also mean responsibility. I want to use my skills to protect systems and find problems. I think permission and following the law are very important in cybersecurity.
