## Findings

### 1. The incident in my own words

The SolarWinds attack was an attack on the supply chain. Hackers have penetrated the SolarWinds development process and added malware to Orion software updates. These updates were sent to the buyers, so that the attackers were able to reach many organizations through which he was trusted. I think the most unusual thing is that the attackers were able to add malware to regular updates that users trusted.

### 2. Who was affected, and how?

Most of the customers and SolarWinds itself were affected. About 18,000 organizations have installed the infected Orion update. Among them were government organizations. This attack also had wider consequences because it became a major international cybersecurity incident and the US government later linked this attack to Russian intelligence. This attack was not just a problem for one company, because the infected SolarWinds update was installed by a large number of organizations. Therefore, one attack through a vendor affected many other organizations.

### 3. The CIA principle

The principle of Confidentiality and Integrity was touched upon here. The Availability was untouched because access to this data was still open. The attackers wanted to secretly collect information and log into the systems instead of destroying data or forgiving ransom. They wanted to gain access to the systems and collect information from the organizations that had installed the infected update. The attackers changed the legal software before it was sent to customers.

### 4. The attack technique - what made this one different?

A supply chain attack means attacking a company through a product or service that many organizations use. One compromised vendor can give attackers access to multiple buyers at the same time. People still installed this software because they trusted the Orion software update. Such an attack differs in that the attackers do not have to attack each company separately. They can compromise one supplier and get into many organizations at once through his product.

### 5. How was it discovered?

The attack was discovered after FireEye noticed unusual activity linked to an employee's multi-factor authentication. The employee couldn't recognize the login, so FireEye launched an investigation and found a major attack. Suspicious activity by one person led to the discovery of a large attack, because FireEye began investigating this unusual login and found other signs of an attack. This story shows that cyber attacks can be detected due to one small suspicious event. Therefore, it is important to notice and check for unusual activity.

### 6. What could have helped?

Strict verification of the software structure could help. I would choose this protection because the attackers were able to add malicious code to the update creation process. A stricter check might have helped to notice this earlier. The company could use a single verification to verify that the final software matches the original source code. Improved monitoring after the update is installed could also help to notice unusual activity already in customers' systems.

### 7. The broader lesson

The SolarWinds attack showed that software updates themselves can become part of the attack. I learned what it is in general, its importance in the infrastructure of companies and that it needs to be monitored constantly. Because suppliers are usually very much trusted, and they don't expect to be framed. You should always keep an eye on the supply chain in your company.

### 8. My personal takeaway

I was surprised how the hackers were able to pull this off so smoothly. Yes, I'm going to be much more attentive to updates now. Yes, now I'm going to think about who created the program and where the updates come from. I will check more carefully where the update is coming from and who created it before installing it. The main idea is you should always update your software, and also understand that updates can create serious security risks.
